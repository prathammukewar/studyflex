how this works

Every card here is a template, not a fact. When a card comes up, its numbers are drawn fresh, so the integral you set up last week comes back with different coefficients and the only thing worth remembering is the method. Type your answer and it gets checked properly: 1/2, 0.5 and sqrt(2)/2 are all read as math, and for indefinite integrals any answer off by a constant counts.

Scheduling is spaced repetition (FSRS, the algorithm behind modern Anki). The app grades you from what actually happened: wrong is again, right but slow or with a hint is hard, right and quick is easy. You can override the grade before moving on. Cards you miss come back a few problems later until they stick, and those practice reps never touch the schedule.

Keys: enter submits and continues, h shows a hint, 1–4 override the grade, esc ends the session early, and ? shows the whole map. Everything is saved in your browser, and nothing leaves it unless you turn on sync under stats.

how templates work

Params draw fresh values each review. Types: int (min, max, optional ne list to exclude values), float (min, max, dp), pick (one record from a list, reach into it with {{q.field}}), and computed (an expr over earlier params, for clean displays). {{a:+}} writes the value with its sign, for building polynomials.

Answer types: number (expr evaluated with the drawn params), expression (expr plus vars; checked by sampling, so any equivalent form counts; set upToConstant for indefinite integrals and domain to keep samples away from singularities), choice (options plus a correct index), and self (you grade yourself, for derivations). Optional display is the pretty form shown after answering. Saving draws the card 100 times and makes the reference answer pass its own checker, so a broken card cannot reach a session.
